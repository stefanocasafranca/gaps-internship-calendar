Live journey map: butcher paper on the wall, Stefano scribes while everyone else takes notes. Neutral line down the middle, post-its above it for her high-momentum moments and below it for her hardest. Post-its, not marker, so they move. Member-check at the end: "Nancy, did I get this right?" She moves what is wrong, in front of the interns. Photograph the map. The LEGO movement is ephemeral; the map is the artifact teams return to all semester. "I Believe That..." (50 Hypotheses) A Capital Factory founder strategy: Nancy verbalizes her assumptions as "I believe that..." statements (e.g., "I believe that an agent that drafts grants will boost the facility"), then the group pressure-tests and debunks them. The point: a founder's real problem is often not what they think it is. Nancy writes her first ~25 in advance and lets them sit, so this block works the harder second half. The first 25 come easily; the real ones need digging. Ladder the why on each one: more grants out quickly, why does that matter, better odds, and then what, double the funding contributes twice as much to society through biotechnology. Keep coming back to why. Tell the newsletter story: a team was asked to build an alumni newsletter, stopped to name the assumption that people wanted to stay in touch, called six alumni and found they did want contact but not a newsletter. Naming the assumption is how you avoid building something nobody uses.

Name each cluster, then write one statement per cluster that captures it, e.g. "reaching more constituents would generate valuable medical research." Ground every statement in two things: what Nancy said was hard, and the assumptions she holds. No solutioning yet: this is Diamond 1, problem-finding only.
